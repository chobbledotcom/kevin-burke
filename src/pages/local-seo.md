## Show up when people search near them

Most local customers search on their phone, look at the map results and the first few websites, and contact one of them. Local SEO is about making sure your business is one of those names, in every town you serve and not just the one on your address.

### What local SEO involves

- **Google Business Profile.** The right categories, services and service areas, good photos, regular posts and questions answered, so your profile has every chance of reaching the map results. I've written a [step-by-step guide for hire firms](/bouncy-castle-google-business-profile-guide/) if you want to see the detail.
- **Reviews.** A simple routine for asking happy customers for a review, and sensible replies to the ones you get. More on this in my [reviews guide](/bouncy-castle-reviews-guide/).
- **Area pages.** A useful page for each main town or district you cover, with local detail that a template can't fake. See [how I write town pages](/bouncy-castle-town-pages-guide/).
- **Consistent business details.** Your name, address, phone number and website the same everywhere they appear, from Yell and Bing Places to industry directories.
- **Local links.** Mentions from local news sites, clubs, schools, suppliers and events you're involved with, which also send real visitors.
- **On-page basics.** Page titles, headings, opening hours, contact details and internal links that tell Google where you are and what you do.

### Two kinds of local business

| Type | What matters most |
|---|---|
| You have premises customers visit | Your address on Google Maps, opening hours, photos of the place, reviews |
| You travel to customers or deliver | Service areas, a page for each town you cover, reviews that mention places |

Hire companies, trades and mobile services are in the second group. They often rank well near their base and nowhere else, which is usually fixable with better area pages and a properly set-up profile.

### Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>Can I rank in a town where I don't have an address?</summary>

In the normal results, yes, with a good page for that town. The map results lean heavily on distance from your address, so they're harder outside your home area, but a strong profile and plenty of reviews help.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Should I use a virtual office address to appear in more towns?</summary>

No. Google's guidelines don't allow it, and profiles using them can be suspended. It's safer to set service areas and build good pages for each town.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do directory listings still help?</summary>

The well-known ones do, mainly by confirming your business details. A handful of accurate listings on sites that suit your trade is worth more than hundreds of low-quality ones.

</details>
