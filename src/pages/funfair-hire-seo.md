## Reach the people who book funfairs

Funfair and ride hire customers range from parents planning a big birthday to school PTAs, councils, festival organisers and corporate event planners. Each of them searches differently, so your website needs pages that speak to each one.

### What I do for funfair and ride hire companies

- **Ride and attraction pages** for waltzers, carousels, dodgems, teacups, children's rides, side stalls, games and food units.
- **Event pages** for school fairs, fun days, festivals, council events, corporate days and private parties.
- **Coverage pages** for the regions or countries you travel to, structured so Google understands your reach.
- **Content that answers planners' questions:** space and power needs, staffing, safety documents and how bookings work.
- **Links and press releases** to build authority, for example when you supply a big local event.

I already list funfair and ride hire companies on [my directory network](/directory-network/). [See how I work](/how-i-work/).


### An example site structure

I start every project by planning the site's structure, then build each page around it. Every business is different, but a typical plan looks something like this:

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Home page | your rides, stalls and the areas you travel to |
| Ride pages | one page per ride or attraction, with space and power needs |
| Stall and side show pages | hook-a-duck, coconut shy, candy floss, popcorn |
| Event pages | council events, school fairs, corporate family days, weddings |
| Area pages | the regions you regularly work in |
| Guides | planning a funfair, safety certificates, what space you need |

Once those foundations are in place, I move on to guides, link building and, where it makes sense, press releases. [Read more about how I work](/how-i-work/).
